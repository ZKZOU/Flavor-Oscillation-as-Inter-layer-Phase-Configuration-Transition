
# Flavor Oscillation as Inter-layer Phase Configuration Transition

 This paper, based on the MITC model proposed by ZOU 2025, proposes that flavor is the overall phase configuration among the internal layered structures of a particle. Motion-induced spatial deformation drives the transition of this configuration, which manifests macroscopically as flavor change.

---

## 1. Introduction

Based on the MITC (Multiplicative-Entropy-Driven Two-Layer Topologically Invariant Fixed-Chirality Substrate Space Model) proposed by ZOU 2025, this paper attempts to provide an intuitive physical perspective on the long-standing problem of flavor oscillation.

In the Standard Model, neutrino flavor oscillation is described as quantum mechanical interference among three mass eigenstates. While mathematically precise, this framework does not reveal the physical carrier of the process itself: when a particle evolves from one flavor to another, what exactly is changing? The MITC model offers a specific answer: particles are not point-like entities, but multi-layered dynamical structures composed of Space Elementary Quanta (SEQ), and "flavor" is precisely the overall phase configuration among the layers of this structure.

## 2. Flavor as Inter-layer Phase Configuration

Within the MITC framework, space possesses an immutable topologically invariant connectivity structure, and its substrate spin has a fixed chirality. Microscopic particles, as excited states of this spatial network, are described as multi-layered, multi-axial synchronously rotating dynamical structural matrices. Each layer carries its own characteristic resonant frequency, and the layers maintain specific phase differences among themselves — this complete distribution pattern of phase differences is the physical carrier of "flavor."

For the same set of layers, when the phase difference combination takes one set of values, it manifests as electron flavor; when the phase difference evolves and slides to another set of values, it manifests as muon flavor or tau flavor.

## 3. The Driving Mechanism: Spatial Deformation and Phase Sliding

Furthermore, the MITC model provides a specific driving mechanism for why phase configuration transitions occur. The core dynamical principle of this model is: energy flows irreversibly along gradients from higher-energy nodes to lower-energy nodes. When a neutrino moves at high velocity, the space ahead and behind it undergoes inhomogeneous compression and stretching. This inhomogeneous deformation differentially modulates the resonant frequencies of its internal layers.

Because different layers experience different degrees of perturbation, their oscillatory rhythms change unevenly, and the inter-layer phase differences begin to undergo cumulative relative sliding. This sliding process is precisely the continuous transition of flavor from one configuration to another.

## 4. CP Violation and Fixed Chirality

This mechanism naturally incorporates another core element of the MITC model: the fixed-chirality spatial substrate. Because the ground-state spin of SEQ possesses a definite chirality, the coupling between left-handed and right-handed layers differs. Consequently, neutrinos and antineutrinos inherently exhibit different rates of phase sliding under the same spatial deformation field. This provides a geometric origin for matter-antimatter asymmetry (CP violation) that requires no additional free parameters.

## 5. Observational Consequences

From an observational standpoint, when this inter-layer configuration transitions to a specific phase combination, the particle's coupling mode with matter via weak interactions changes accordingly. The "flavor change" recorded by the detector is essentially a "readout" of the current inter-layer phase configuration.

In the low-energy flat-space limit, the accumulated phase difference of this mechanism degenerates into the standard oscillation behavior driven by mass-squared differences; whereas at high energies or over long baselines, the nonlinear modulation of layer frequencies by spatial deformation will lead to testable deviations in the oscillation probability.

## 6. Conclusion

Thus, viewing neutrino flavor oscillation as an inter-layer phase configuration transition does not negate the mathematical framework of the Standard Model. Rather, it offers an intuitive answer — grounded in the MITC model — to the question of "what is evolving and how" that lies behind the formalism.
